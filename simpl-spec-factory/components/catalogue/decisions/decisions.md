# Decisions (step 30a): catalogue

Decisions that close the findings of steps 20b ([`ac-findings.json`](../validation/ac-findings.json)) and 23b ([`domain-findings.json`](../validation/domain-findings.json)) for the catalogue build, and since 17 Nov 2026 those of step 21b ([`api-findings.json`](../validation/api-findings.json)) and step 22b ([`bp-findings.json`](../validation/bp-findings.json)). Full text, options and basis per decision are in [`decisions.json`](./decisions.json); the final glossary is in [`glossary.md`](./glossary.md). The spec owners were not available, so most decisions are provisional and carry a question for the owner.

## Source hierarchy

When sources disagree, the higher one wins:

1. These decisions.
2. Live acceptance criteria, for behaviour. Struck text never applies, but a later strike shows the owner's intent and is used as such (D29).
3. The entry OpenAPI files, for the wire: paths, field names, status codes, error bodies. Where a contract has a gap (an operation not yet defined), a decision fills it in the same style.
4. Technical details, notes and hints in the stories.
5. The CDMs in `sources.yaml`, for names and ownership.
6. The business processes BP05B and BP06.
7. Non-entry API files (catalogue-client-be v1, `*-itb`), only as context when the entry files have nothing (D23, D32).

Why: criteria are what the tests check; the OpenAPI is what the code must serve; notes are design hints from one moment; the CDMs and processes are drafts and broader than this build. The `API-`, `DDD-` and `SEC-` rules shape every new or changed contract.
